Shared feedback
1. Consonant repetition + rhythm unit separation
: "dun-dun," "da-ra-ra," "du-ru-du-da" → break the rhythm into sound units and repeat consonants

2. Express pitch or sustain with vowel changes
: "da-da-da" (short and low), "da-ra-ra," "ta-ka-ta-ka" (rising and rhythmic)

3. Intentional overlap and emphasis
: "ba-ba-ba-ba / ba-ba ba-ba" → spacing or emphasis placement can convey different feelings

4. Drum syllables have some fixed patterns
: "kung-chi-tta-chi," "dung-ku-dung-ku-tta" — when conventional patterns exist, make active use of them

5. Reflect the transient-resonance concept in structure
: "dung" equals a low-pitched resonance, "ddak/tak" is a sharp transient attack

6. Strategy for turning rhythm units into text
- Break by number of notes or beats
: eighth notes → repeat 2 syllables ("da-da"), sixteenth notes → fast consonant repetition ("ta-ta-ta-ta")
- Group repeating patterns into meaningful units
: "da-ra-ra," "da-ra-ra-ra"

7. Timbre segmentation elements
- Transient → consonants (percussive sounds): t, d, p, ch, tt, tak, ddak, etc.
- Resonance → vowels/final consonants (reverb/afterglow): u, eu, ng, m, etc.
: "tak" = transient + resonance combined into one syllable

8. How to indicate beat/accent differences
- Use spaces, repeated expressions, or strong consonants (double consonants)
: "ppa-ba ppa-ba" (repeat twice), "ppa-ppa-ppa" (emphasis)

9. Role of 'r' – reinforces phrasing and rhythmic feel
- When 'r' appears between obstruents, it enhances speed and phrase connection.
- Because 'r' is articulated slightly inside, placing it between obstruents helps express groove or tempo.
: di-ri-da-ra-tta-ra-tu, chi-ri-ti-ri-ta-ra-tta-ra-ra-tu

10. Role of 'h' – soft attack and airy expression in high registers
- 'h' suits wind noise, gentle friction sounds, or lingering connections.
(ha, hwi, hi-ing, etc. → expected to work for clarinet, flute, or cymbal expressions; add more after verification)
