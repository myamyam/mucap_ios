— Summary of Key Findings —
1. Repetition of consonants + rhythmic segmentation
Participants commonly represented rhythm by breaking sounds into units and repeating consonants:
Examples: “dun-dun,” “da-ra-ra,” “du-ru-du-tta”

2. Vowel changes used to express pitch or duration
Short vowels indicated short/low sounds (“da-da-da”), while varying vowels expressed rising or rhythmic patterns (“ta-ka-ta-ka,” “ta-ra-ra”).

3. Intentional layering and stress expression
Spacing or grouping influenced perceived emphasis:
Examples: “ba-ba-ba-ba” vs. “ba-ba ba-ba”

4. Drum syllables exhibit semi-standardized patterns
Conventional vocal percussion patterns exist and should be utilized when possible:
Examples: “kung-chi-tta-chi,” “dung-ku-dung-ku-tta”

5. Structural mapping of transient–resonance concepts
“dung” corresponds to a low-pitched resonant sound
“tak/tak” corresponds to a sharp transient attack

6. Strategy for text-based rhythm representation
Divide syllables based on note values or beats
Eighth notes → two-syllable repetition (“da-da”)
Sixteenth notes → fast consonant repetition (“tta-tta-tta-tta”)
Meaningful rhythmic patterns are grouped
“ta-ra-ra,” “da-ra-ra-ra”

7. Timbre-based decomposition
Transient → consonants (percussive hits): t, d, p, ch, tt, tak, ttak
Resonance → vowels/finals (sustain/aftertone): u, eu, ng, m
Example: “tak” = transient + resonance combined

8. Distinguishing beats and accents
Accents can be shown through spacing, repetition, or strong consonants (double consonants):
Examples: “ppa-ba ppa-ba”, “ppa-ppa-ppa”

9. Role of ‘ㄹ’ — enhances phrasing and rhythmic flow
When inserted between obstruents, ‘ㄹ’ adds speed, continuity, and phrase connection; it naturally conveys internal motion:
Examples: “di-ri-da-ra-tta-ra-ttu,” “chi-ri-ti-ri-ta-ra-tta-ra-ra-tu”

10. Role of ‘ㅎ’ — soft attack and airy high-frequency expression
‘ㅎ’ effectively conveys breathy or soft attack qualities, suitable for representing flutes, clarinets, cymbals, or air-like timbres:
Examples: “ha,” “hwi,” “hi-ing” — with potential for further refinement.
